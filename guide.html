Build guide · no soldering · about 45 minutes

Fridge Monitor

Two probes measure the fridge and the freezer. A display on the outside shows both temperatures in turn. On your phone, via the monitor's own Wi-Fi, you can see the history of the last 7 days.

This is what the display looks like. It has no real degree sign: the small square at the top right is made of the four upper segments of the last digit. It has no decimal point either, so from 0.0 to 9.9 °C it shows one decimal and uses the lower-left segment as the decimal mark, as in 4.5 °C above. Everything else is shown in whole degrees. The colon always stays off.

Getting to know the ESP32 board

This is your board viewed from above: antenna at the top, USB-C at the bottom. Next to each pin its name is printed on the board. You only need the coloured ones. Almost all of them are on the right-hand side.

Careful: “16” is labelled “RX2”

The pin the firmware uses as GPIO 16 is labelled RX2 on this board. It is on the right between TX2 and D4.

1

Both probes on one adapter board

Both probes connect to the same adapter board. Each terminal takes two wires of the same colour.

  1. Open the terminal screws a few turns.
  2. Put the red wire of probe A and the red wire of probe B together into the VCC terminal (sometimes “+” or “V”). Tighten the screw.
  3. Do the same with both yellow wires in DAT (sometimes “DQ”, “S” or “OUT”). If the data wire is white or blue, it goes here.
  4. Both black wires into GND (sometimes “–”).
  5. Gently tug on each wire to make sure it's secure. None may slip out.
Why only one board?

Both probes share the same data line. Each has its own serial number, which is how the ESP32 tells them apart. This means you only need three wires between the adapter and the ESP32.

2

Adapter board to the ESP32

Three jumper wires, all on the right-hand side of the board.

Why does the probe power come from RX2?

RX2 is just a name. Behind it is the ordinary pin GPIO 16. It is called that because it can serve as the receive line of a serial port. The firmware uses it as an output instead and switches it on. Then it carries 3.3 V, just like the 3V3 pin.

Can an output pin supply power? Yes, for small currents. An ESP32 pin easily delivers 20 mA. Both probes together need only 2–3 mA. It is not enough for the display, which draws up to 80 mA and therefore sits on the real 3V3 pin. There is only one of those, and each pin takes only one wire.

Bonus: since the ESP32 switches the probe power itself, it can turn stuck probes off and on again. It does this automatically when it finds no probe three times in a row, or when a probe delivers no valid readings for 3 minutes.

Don't confuse these pins

RX0 and TX0 (right, 3rd and 4th pin) belong to the USB connection. Don't connect anything there.

Tip

Use red or orange for power and black for GND. Then you can see at a glance what goes where when you check.

4

Check before powering up

A two-minute check that can save you trouble later. Swapped wires are the only mistake that can really break something.

  • Probe VCC is on RX2, not on 3V3.
  • Display VCC is on 3V3, not on VIN.
  • No GND wire is on a power pin, and vice versa.
  • All seven jumper wires are fully seated on their pins, none is crooked.
  • No bare wire ends touch each other at the adapter board.
5

Flash the firmware

You only need to do this once, using a PC with Chrome or Edge. Later updates go over Wi-Fi.

File

You need the file kuehlschrank-full.bin. Download it from the GitHub release.

  1. Connect the ESP32 to the PC with the USB cable. A red LED lights up on the board.
  2. Open this page in Chrome or Edge:
    https://espressif.github.io/esptool-js/open
  3. Set the baud rate (Baudrate) to 460800 and click Connect. In the dialog, pick the entry with “CP210x”.
  4. If it keeps saying “Connecting…”: hold the BOOT button (right of the USB socket) until it continues.
  5. Under “Program”, enter 0x0 as Flash Address and select the file kuehlschrank-full.bin.
  6. Click Program and wait until it reaches 100 % (about 30 seconds).
  7. Press the EN button (left of the USB socket), or unplug the USB cable briefly and plug it back in.
No port in the list?

Then the USB driver “CP210x” from Silicon Labs is missing. Windows usually installs it by itself; otherwise install it once from the Silicon Labs website and reload the browser. Also check that the cable carries data. Charge-only cables don't work.

6

First start

Here's what you'll see when everything is working.

Screenshots with example data.

  1. The display briefly shows AP and then 192 168 4 1, the address of the web page. After that, the temperatures alternate.
  2. On the phone, choose the Wi-Fi network Kuehlschrank, password kuehlschrank.
  3. The web page usually opens automatically. Otherwise type 192.168.4.1 into the browser.
  4. If the phone asks “This network has no internet. Stay connected?”: Yes.
  5. Hold the metal tip of one probe in your hand. After a few seconds its value rises towards 30 °C. That's how you know which probe is which.
Names

A probe reading below −5 °C is automatically named “Gefrierfach” (freezer), otherwise “Kühlschrank” (fridge). On the first start on the table, both are called “Kühlschrank”. On the web page under Einstellungen (settings) you can rename or swap them. The web page is in German.

Set your own password

The default hotspot password “kuehlschrank” is publicly available. On the web page under WLAN → Hotspot, enter your own password (at least 8 characters). Then reconnect the phone with the new password.

Home Wi-Fi (optional)

Under WLAN (Wi-Fi), pick your home Wi-Fi and enter its password. The display then shows IP and the new address; http://kuehlschrank.local works too. After about a minute the hotspot switches off and the page is only reachable on the home Wi-Fi. If the home Wi-Fi fails, the hotspot is back after 2 minutes.

7

Installing it on the fridge

Keep the electronics outside the fridge. Only the probes go inside.

  1. Display and ESP32 go on the outside, on the side or on top of the fridge, with Velcro. Not on the metal back panel: it gets warm there and the Wi-Fi is weak.
  2. Place the fridge probe on the middle shelf, near the front or centre. Not against the back wall, which gets ice-cold while cooling and distorts the reading.
  3. Place the freezer probe among the frozen food, without burying it, and not right in front of the fan or against the wall.
  4. Inside, tuck the cable behind the edge of a shelf or door shelf. Tape sticks badly to cold, damp inner walls.
  5. Outside, secure it with tape, see below.
Smoother readings

A probe measuring air temperature shows a spike whenever the door opens. If you put the metal tip of the fridge probe into a small glass of water, it measures something closer to the food temperature and the history gets smoother.

Cable through the door

This is the usual way. The probe cable is round and about 4 mm thick. The soft magnetic seal wraps around it. Choosing the right spot and checking the seal afterwards are what matter.

  1. Pass it through on the hinge side. The seal barely moves there when the door opens. On the handle side it rubs over the cable every time.
  2. Near the top or bottom of the hinge side, not halfway up the door. That's where the seal presses most evenly.
  3. Lay the cable flat, not twisted and without a loop inside the seal.
  4. Paper test: close the door with a sheet of paper trapped right next to the cable. If it slides out without resistance, the seal leaks there. Move the cable a few centimetres.
  5. Check again after 2–3 days: frost or condensation on the cable means warm air is getting in there.

Should the cable be taped down outside?

Yes. The tape takes the strain off the cable. If someone catches it when opening the door, the probe would otherwise be pulled out of the adapter terminal.

  • Tape the cable to the cabinet (the side wall), 5–10 cm past the seal. Not to the door and not to the seal: the door moves, and adhesive residue on the seal makes it leak.
  • Leave some slack between seal and tape so the door doesn't pull the cable tight when it opens.
  • Add another piece of tape just before the electronics so the weight of the cable doesn't hang on the jumper wires.
  • Masking tape or duct tape works well. Masking tape peels off painted surfaces later without residue.

Strip the cable jacket where it passes the seal?

Under the grey jacket there are three thin wires. Without the jacket they lie flat side by side, about 1.5 mm instead of 4 mm, and the seal closes better. But it has downsides.

Try without first

The round cable works with many fridge door seals. If the paper test passes and there is no frost on the cable after a few days, leave the jacket on.

Only if it doesn't seal

Only then remove the outer jacket where the cable passes through the seal. An adult should do this, see below.

If the jacket comes off, do it like this
  • Only a short piece, about 6–8 cm, exactly where the cable lies in the seal.
  • Use wire strippers or a cable stripping tool. With a utility knife, only score lengthwise and very shallowly, never cut around. A cut into one of the wires ruins the probe.
  • Check every wire afterwards: wherever copper shows, wrap that spot with electrical tape.
  • Lay the three wires flat side by side and bundle them into a flat ribbon with one layer of electrical tape. Wrap the cut ends of the jacket with electrical tape too.
  • The individual wires are more delicate without the outer jacket. That makes the tape outside especially important, so there is no pull on them.
Freezer

Here even a small gap becomes a problem faster, because ice builds up. Do the paper test on the freezer door especially carefully, and watch for frost at the cable during the first week.

Don't drill

Holes in the cabinet or door are a bad idea. Refrigerant lines run inside the walls. The seal is the right place.

When something doesn't work

Display doesn't turn on

Check the display wires: VCC to 3V3, GND to GND (left), DIO to D23, CLK to D18. Is the firmware installed? Without firmware the display stays dark.

Display shows ----

The probe isn't delivering readings. Check: adapter VCC to RX2, DAT to D4, GND to GND, and the wires sit tight in the terminals. The web page says “Fühler nicht gefunden” (probe not found).

Only one probe is detected

One of the second probe's wires may not be seated properly in its terminal. Tighten all three terminals. The ESP32 checks for probes again every 30 seconds.

Wi-Fi “Kuehlschrank” is missing

Check the USB cable and charger. Is the red LED on the ESP32 lit? Briefly press the EN button.

The web page won't open

Type 192.168.4.1 into the browser by hand. Briefly turn off mobile data on the phone in case it looks for the page over the mobile network.

Forgot the address

Briefly press the BOOT button. The display shows the address again (AP = hotspot, IP = home Wi-Fi).

Forgot the password, or start over

Factory reset: hold the BOOT button for 10 seconds. The display counts down rES7 to rES1, then the ESP32 restarts. After restarting, the hotspot will be named “Kuehlschrank” with the password “kuehlschrank” again. Letting go earlier cancels.

History is empty

After a power cut the history starts over. It fills up with one value per minute.
